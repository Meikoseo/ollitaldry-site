Atomization Detail

Pneumatic Two-Fluid Nozzle

Separate liquid and compressed-air or inert-gas passages meet at the nozzle tip, allowing droplet breakup to be tuned without relying on feed pressure alone.

How It Works

The high-velocity gas shears the liquid as it exits the tip. Gas-to-liquid ratio, pressure and tip geometry shape the spray angle and the resulting droplet distribution.

Primary Control Variables

  • Atomizing-gas pressure and flow
  • Liquid feed rate and viscosity
  • Liquid and gas passage geometry
  • Nozzle position within the drying chamber

Typical Application Direction

Useful for laboratory development, lower feed rates, fine-particle direction and materials that benefit from a broad adjustment window.

Engineering Considerations

Compressed-gas demand, nozzle wear and the possibility of a broader distribution must be reviewed against the target powder and chamber duty.

Atomization Detail

High-Pressure Nozzle

A single liquid stream passes through an internal swirl chamber and calibrated orifice, converting feed pressure into a controlled spray without a separate atomizing-gas connection.

How It Works

Pressure energy and internal swirl generate a hollow-cone pattern. The selected orifice and operating pressure are coordinated with viscosity, solids content and required feed rate.

Primary Control Variables

  • Feed pressure at the nozzle
  • Orifice size and internal swirl geometry
  • Feed viscosity and solids content
  • Filtration and allowable wear rate

Typical Application Direction

Suited to pumpable, reasonably uniform feeds where a robust single-fluid route and medium-to-high throughput direction are required.

Engineering Considerations

Pressure rating, blockage risk, erosion and changes in viscosity require engineering review before the nozzle and pump duty are fixed.

Atomization Detail

Rotary (Centrifugal) Atomizer

Feed reaches a high-speed wheel or disc and is accelerated radially to the edge, where the liquid film breaks into droplets. Rotary atomizer and centrifugal atomizer describe the same technology.

How It Works

Wheel speed, edge design and feed loading establish the liquid-film behavior and initial droplet distribution, producing a broad radial spray suited to compatible chamber geometry.

Primary Control Variables

  • Atomizer wheel or disc speed
  • Feed rate and liquid distribution to the wheel
  • Wheel diameter and edge design
  • Feed rheology, cleaning and balance condition

Typical Application Direction

Applied where stable feeds, higher capacity and a wide operating window justify a mechanically driven atomizer and the matching chamber layout.

Engineering Considerations

Precision balancing, specialist maintenance, cleaning access and potential wall deposition must be considered as part of the complete dryer design.

Questions Before You Test

Frequently Asked Questions

What particle-size range can spray drying achieve?+

A broad range is possible, but the practical target depends on material properties, atomizer type, solids content, viscosity and process conditions.

How is particle-size consistency controlled?+

Feed preparation, atomization energy, droplet formation, temperature, airflow and collection are controlled as one process.

Can you control morphology as well as size?+

Yes. Hollow or dense particles, surface texture and agglomeration behavior may be influenced through formulation and process design.

Do you provide pilot testing?+

Yes. Pilot trials can compare atomization directions and operating windows before scale-up.

Which analytical methods are available?+

Depending on the project, review may include laser diffraction, sieving, microscopy, moisture, bulk density and flowability.

Can a particle-size result be scaled up?+

Pilot data helps reduce risk, but final scale-up also requires review of atomizer geometry, air distribution, residence time and collection behavior.

What information is needed to define a particle-size target?+

Useful starting information includes the intended powder application, existing particle-size data, acceptable fines and oversize limits, feed composition, solids content, viscosity, throughput and downstream handling requirements.

How is distribution span calculated?+

For a volume-based distribution, span is commonly calculated as (D90 - D10) / D50. The calculation is only meaningful when the sampling method, dispersion procedure and analytical settings are controlled and reported.

Can spray drying reduce fines or oversized particles?+

Atomizer selection, atomization energy, feed condition and air handling can shift the distribution, while collection and classification also influence the recovered powder. The practical improvement must be established through trials with representative material.

How do viscosity and solids content affect particle size?+

Higher viscosity can resist droplet breakup, while solids content changes both viscosity and the amount of dry matter in each droplet. Their combined effect is reviewed with feed temperature, atomizer settings and pumpability.
