Answers Before You Start

Frequently Asked Questions

What information do you need to start a project?+

We begin with the feed properties, target powder, required capacity, available utilities, site constraints and applicable standards.

Do you provide installation and commissioning?+

Yes. The project scope can include installation guidance, commissioning, operator training and handover documentation.

How long does a turnkey plant project take?+

Timing depends on testing, process complexity, compliance requirements, fabrication scope and destination. A project schedule is confirmed after engineering review.

Can you integrate with existing systems?+

Existing utilities, upstream preparation, downstream handling and control interfaces can be reviewed for project-specific integration.

What documentation is included?+

The agreed package can include process diagrams, layouts, equipment data, utility schedules, manuals, inspection records and commissioning documents.

Do you support international standards?+

Applicable CE, GMP, ATEX, IECEx and local requirements can enter the engineering review when specified at project start.

How do you determine the correct plant capacity?+

Capacity is developed from the feed solids, inlet and outlet conditions, evaporation duty, target powder output, operating schedule and expected production growth. Pilot data is used where available.

Can the plant be designed for future expansion?+

Yes. The initial engineering review can reserve footprint, utility capacity, control-system interfaces and modular connection points for an agreed future production stage.

Is pilot testing required before detailed engineering?+

Pilot testing is recommended when material behavior, atomization, thermal sensitivity, wall deposition or collection efficiency has not yet been demonstrated on representative feed.

Can you include CIP and automated controls?+

CIP coverage, recipe control, data logging, alarm handling and safety interlocks can be included when they are defined in the project scope and process-risk review.

How are utilities and site constraints reviewed?+

We review the available steam or heat source, electrical supply, compressed air, water, cooling, exhaust route, floor loading, headroom, access and destination-site regulations.

What happens during factory acceptance testing?+

The agreed FAT plan can cover equipment inspection, documentation review, control and interlock checks, dry running and recorded punch-list closure before shipment.
