Fruit Powder

Mango Powder

Reduced wall buildup and improved yield.

Material Challenge

Mango concentrate combines natural sugars, organic acids and high soluble solids. As droplets lose moisture, their surfaces can remain soft and tacky, increasing deposits on the chamber wall and losses through the collection stage.

Engineering Approach

  • Screened feed-solids and viscosity ranges to establish a stable pumping and atomization window.
  • Balanced droplet size with inlet and outlet conditions so particles dried before reaching critical wall-contact zones.
  • Refined airflow, powder discharge and post-drying cooling to reduce recirculation, agglomeration and collection losses.

Result

The optimized window supported a longer stable run with less visible wall deposition, fewer cleaning interruptions and a higher proportion of recoverable, free-flowing mango powder.

+18%Yield Increase
-45%Cleaning Frequency

Representative project outcome. Final performance depends on feed properties, test scope and the confirmed process configuration.
Herbal Extract

Turmeric Extract Powder

Consistent quality with fewer shutdowns.

Material Challenge

Turmeric extract can combine variable viscosity, hygroscopic soluble solids and fine insoluble particles. These characteristics may disturb atomization, create wet wall deposits and cause inconsistent color, moisture and powder recovery.

Engineering Approach

  • Improved feed preparation and homogenization to maintain a repeatable solids distribution throughout the run.
  • Selected an atomization direction that handled the extract viscosity while limiting oversized wet droplets.
  • Controlled outlet moisture, chamber-wall exposure and cyclone separation to protect powder quality and keep discharge stable.

Result

More consistent atomization and powder discharge reduced unplanned stops and allowed a higher, steadier feed rate while maintaining the required turmeric-powder condition.

+22%Throughput Increase
-40%Downtime

Protein

Whey Protein Concentrate

Improved flowability and reduced caking.

Material Challenge

Concentrated whey feeds contain proteins, lactose and minerals that can become adhesive under unsuitable moisture and temperature conditions. Excess thermal exposure may also affect solubility, while warm powder can cake during collection.

Engineering Approach

  • Matched feed concentration and temperature with pump delivery to keep viscosity stable before atomization.
  • Adjusted droplet formation, residence time and outlet conditions to limit wet deposition and unnecessary heat exposure.
  • Applied faster powder removal and cooling with controlled collection to improve flowability and reduce post-drying caking.

Result

The combined process direction improved recovered yield and increased the share of powder meeting the intended moisture, handling and reconstitution requirements.

FAQs

What makes a material sticky during spray drying?+

Stickiness often develops when the particle surface remains above its glass-transition temperature or absorbs moisture. Sugars, organic acids, proteins and botanical solids can all increase wall deposition risk.

How can wall buildup be reduced?+

The engineering review can combine chamber geometry, inlet and outlet conditions, atomization, air handling, cooling, collection and a suitable cleaning strategy.

Can you improve yield on existing equipment?+

Yes. Feed behavior, operating data, deposits and collection losses can be reviewed to identify practical process and equipment changes.

Is pilot testing recommended?+

Pilot testing is strongly recommended when the feed is hygroscopic, high in sugar, viscous or prone to thermal softening.

Which materials are commonly evaluated?+

Fruit concentrates, botanical extracts, protein feeds, carbohydrates, flavors and other high-solids formulations are common candidates.

Do you support scale-up and optimization?+

Yes. Laboratory and pilot observations can be translated into chamber, atomization, collection and operating recommendations for the next scale.

How much sample is needed for a sticky-material test?+

Sample quantity depends on solids content, viscosity, the number of conditions to be screened and the selected laboratory or pilot system. We confirm the practical minimum after reviewing your material information.

Which feed properties should we provide before testing?+

Useful information includes composition, solids content, viscosity, density, pH, heat sensitivity, hygroscopicity, solvent basis and any available safety or technical data sheets.

How are inlet and outlet temperatures selected?+

Temperature direction is established from heat sensitivity, stickiness behavior, target moisture and drying response. The operating window is refined during controlled trials rather than selected from one fixed value.

Can cleaning frequency be reduced for sticky feeds?+

Often yes. Chamber surface condition, airflow, atomization, powder cooling, discharge design and CIP coverage can be reviewed together to reduce avoidable deposits and shorten cleaning work.

How is powder recovery evaluated?+

We review powder collected from the chamber, cyclone and filters, along with visible deposits and carryover. This helps identify where material is being lost and which collection changes may improve recovery.

What results are provided after a test?+

The agreed test summary can include operating conditions, observations, recovered yield, moisture and relevant powder characteristics, together with engineer-reviewed recommendations for the next development step.
