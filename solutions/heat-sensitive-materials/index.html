Common Thermal Risks

Heat-sensitive materials can lose quality at surprisingly low temperatures.

Loss of Bioactivity

Enzymes and probiotics can deactivate, reducing efficacy.

Color Degradation

Heat triggers browning, oxidation and visible color shift.

Aroma & Flavor Loss

Volatile compounds evaporate at elevated temperatures.

Viability Reduction

High heat shortens shelf life and reduces viable cultures.

Nutrient Breakdown

Vitamins and sensitive nutrients can degrade.

Our Low-Temperature Drying Strategies

Engineered to minimize heat exposure while maximizing drying efficiency.

Short Residence Time

Spray droplets dry within seconds, minimizing exposure to damaging temperatures. Fast chamber discharge helps protect bioactivity, color and delicate functional ingredients.

Precise Temperature Control

Independent inlet and outlet monitoring keeps the process inside a validated thermal window. Feed rate and airflow can be adjusted before product quality begins to drift.

Advanced Atomization

Purpose-selected nozzles create a controlled droplet distribution for rapid moisture removal. Uniform atomization reduces wet zones without unnecessary thermal exposure.

Advanced Process Control

Questions Before You Test

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of heat-sensitive materials can be evaluated?+

Typical work includes enzymes, probiotics, botanical extracts, dairy ingredients, vitamins, nutraceuticals and pharmaceutical intermediates.

How do you protect bioactivity and viability?+

Short residence time, controlled inlet and outlet temperatures, appropriate atomization and rapid powder removal are reviewed together.

Can the process be customized for our material?+

Yes. Feed composition, solids content, viscosity, target moisture and critical quality attributes guide the process direction.

What is a typical pilot-trial timeline?+

Timing depends on sample availability and the test scope. A test plan and required material quantity are confirmed before scheduling.

Do you support full-scale production?+

Yes. Pilot data can support equipment selection, operating windows and scale-up risk review for production systems.

How is product quality checked?+

Relevant outputs may include moisture, yield, particle size, morphology, color, reconstitution and retained functional activity.

How much sample is required for a low-temperature drying trial?+

Sample quantity depends on solids content, the number of conditions to be screened and whether laboratory or pilot equipment is selected. We confirm the practical minimum after reviewing the feed data.

Can spray drying preserve live probiotic cultures?+

Spray drying can support useful survival when the formulation and thermal window are suitable. Actual viability must be measured during trials because strain, carrier system, moisture and storage conditions all affect the result.

How is the atomizer selected for a heat-sensitive feed?+

Viscosity, solids content, feed behavior, target particle size and allowable thermal exposure are reviewed together. The nozzle or atomizer is selected to create droplets that dry rapidly without unnecessary heat load.

Are carriers or protective excipients always required?+

Not always. Maltodextrin, proteins, gums or other carriers may improve stability, powder formation or recovery, but any formulation change should be evaluated against product requirements and labeling constraints.

What information is included in the trial report?+

The agreed report can include operating conditions, feed observations, powder recovery, moisture, particle characteristics and relevant quality measurements, followed by engineer-reviewed recommendations.

Can an existing spray dryer be optimized for heat-sensitive products?+

Yes. We can review inlet and outlet control, feed rate, atomization, airflow, chamber residence time, powder discharge and collection performance to identify practical improvement directions.
