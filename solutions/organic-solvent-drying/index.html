Representative Application Direction

API Intermediate

Ethanol-based feed

An ethanol-based API intermediate route starts with the complete formulation, solvent-to-water ratio, solids loading, thermal sensitivity and containment target. These inputs determine whether closed-loop spray drying is an appropriate development path.

The engineering concept coordinates inert-gas operation, atomization, low-residence-time drying, condenser duty and contained powder discharge. Cleaning strategy, product-contact materials and operator exposure controls are reviewed as part of the same process boundary.

Engineering Review Focus

  • SDS and formulation review before testing
  • Low-temperature process-window development
  • Contained collection and cleaning compatibility
  • Recovery duty verified from the measured vapor load

Representative Application Direction

Agrochemical Active

Acetone-based feed

Acetone-based agrochemical feeds require a coordinated review of solvent flammability, active-ingredient toxicity, suspension behavior, corrosion risk and the dust characteristics of the recovered powder.

The proposed route can combine sealed feed handling, inert-gas purging, oxygen monitoring, grounded product-contact equipment and staged condensation. Collection and isolation arrangements are selected around powder flow, occupational-exposure requirements and the approved site classification.

Engineering Review Focus

  • Solvent, dust and toxicity hazards assessed together
  • Oxygen monitoring and permissive logic
  • Grounding and compatible seal-material review
  • Condenser stages sized for the project cooling conditions

Representative Application Direction

Natural Extract

Ethyl-acetate feed

Ethyl-acetate botanical and natural-extract feeds may contain heat-sensitive aroma compounds, variable solids and oxidation-sensitive actives. Feed characterization and pilot work are used to establish a stable atomization and drying window.

The process concept emphasizes short residence time, controlled oxygen conditions and condensation matched to the actual vapor composition. Hygienic product-contact surfaces, seal compatibility, cleaning access and gentle powder collection are reviewed before scale-up.

Engineering Review Focus

  • Aroma, color and active retention evaluated in trials
  • Gentle atomization and residence-time control
  • Closed collection limits solvent release and oxidation
  • Hygienic construction and cleaning access reviewed

Questions Before You Test

Frequently Asked Questions

Which organic solvents can be reviewed?+

Ethanol, isopropanol, acetone, ethyl acetate and other solvents may be considered after a complete material, compatibility and safety review.

How are flammable-solvent risks managed?+

The direction may require a closed loop, nitrogen inerting, oxygen monitoring, solvent recovery, pressure protection, suitable electrical classification and interlocks.

Can you design for an ATEX zone?+

ATEX and IECEx requirements can enter the engineering scope. The final protection concept depends on the solvent, concentration, site and destination standards.

Do you provide pilot testing?+

Testing may be possible in a suitably reviewed closed-loop system. The solvent and all hazards must be disclosed before acceptance.

What solvent recovery can be expected?+

Recovery depends on solvent properties, feed load, condenser duty and operating conditions. The target is confirmed during process engineering.

What affects project lead time?+

Safety review, testing, process complexity, compliance documentation and custom fabrication all affect the project schedule.

What information is required before a solvent trial?+

Provide the complete formulation, current SDS documents, solvent concentration, flash point and other available ignition or exposure data, together with the target batch size and powder specification. Trial acceptance follows a formal technical and safety review.

How much sample is required for feasibility testing?+

The amount depends on solids content, atomization route, analytical work and the number of conditions to be assessed. We define the minimum representative quantity after reviewing the feed and test objectives.

How is oxygen concentration controlled in the closed loop?+

A project-specific purge sequence, nitrogen supply, continuous oxygen measurement and permissive logic are coordinated so solvent feeding only begins after the engineered condition is reached. Alarm and shutdown limits are established during the hazard review.

How is the condensation system selected?+

Condenser surface area, staging and coolant temperature are calculated from solvent properties, vapor load, operating pressure and the intended recovery target. Low-temperature or secondary condensation is added only when the duty requires it.

Can the recovered solvent be reused directly?+

Recovered solvent quality can be affected by water, volatile feed components and cross-contamination. Sampling and quality verification are required before reuse, and any purification or disposal route remains subject to the customer process and local regulations.

Can an existing spray dryer be converted for organic solvents?+

A retrofit may be possible, but it is not a simple enclosure change. Chamber tightness, gas circulation, condensation, pressure protection, electrical classification, grounding, instrumentation and control logic all require an engineering assessment before a conversion can be recommended.
