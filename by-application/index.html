Process Configuration

Process Options Configured Around Your Material

These are engineering directions, not one-size-fits-all selections. Feed data and safety requirements determine the approved configuration.

Two-Fluid Nozzle Atomization

Small feed volumes and flexible droplet control.

Compressed-gas atomization may be evaluated for laboratory and pilot applications.

Pressure Nozzle Atomization

Higher feed pressure and larger operating duties.

Pressure atomization can be considered depending on viscosity and production requirements.

Centrifugal Atomization

Flexible throughput and industrial operation.

A rotary atomizer can be evaluated for applicable feeds and target particle properties.

Open-Cycle Drying

Applicable water-based feed systems.

A once-through air system is commonly considered after material and site review.

Closed-Loop Drying

Solvent recovery or controlled atmosphere needs.

Closed-loop operation may be evaluated after process and safety assessment.

Cyclone & Filter Collection

Powder recovery and emission-control requirements.

Collection stages are selected around particle behavior and recovery targets.

CIP Cleaning

Cleaning consistency and product changeover.

Clean-in-place design can be evaluated for the approved process and equipment scope.

Explosion Protection

Combustible dust or applicable solvent risks.

Protection measures require a formal material, process and site safety review.

Application Questions

Common Questions About Spray Drying Different Materials

Can sticky materials be spray dried?+

Some sticky materials can be evaluated with formulation support, a suitable temperature window and an appropriate collection strategy. Feasibility depends on the actual feed.

How are heat-sensitive ingredients protected?+

Feed formulation, inlet and outlet conditions, residence time and atomization are reviewed together. Laboratory testing may be recommended before scale-up.

Can organic solvents be processed in a spray dryer?+

Applicable solvent-based feeds require formal process and safety evaluation. Closed-loop or inert-gas operation may be considered where appropriate.

Which atomization method is suitable for viscous feeds?+

The suitable method depends on viscosity, solids, feed behavior, throughput and particle targets. Nozzle and centrifugal options can be compared during evaluation.

Can one spray dryer process different materials?+

A configurable system may support multiple approved materials, but cleaning, cross-contamination, corrosion and operating-range requirements must be reviewed.

How can powder recovery be improved?+

Recovery can be influenced by formulation, wall sticking, particle size, cyclone or filter selection and operating conditions.

Can laboratory results be scaled up to production?+

Laboratory results establish an initial direction. Pilot validation is often used to confirm longer-run stability, recovery and scale-up assumptions.

What material data is required before equipment selection?+

Useful information includes feed form, solids, viscosity, solvent basis, thermal sensitivity, available quantity, target powder and expected capacity.

How much sample is required for testing?+

Sample quantity depends on the material, test objective and number of conditions. Submit the quantity available for a practical recommendation.

Can corrosive materials be processed?+

Material compatibility and corrosion risk must be reviewed before selecting construction materials and process configuration.

How is cross-contamination controlled?+

Equipment layout, cleaning access, collection design, contact materials and validation requirements are reviewed for the intended products.

What powder properties can be evaluated?+

Relevant properties may include moisture, particle size, flowability, bulk density, solubility, morphology and product recovery.
