Common Mistakes When Choosing Spray Dryer Scale

Choosing Only by Feed Volume

Feed volume does not directly equal evaporation duty. Solids and water-removal requirements must also be considered.

Ignoring Material Properties

Viscosity, stickiness, temperature sensitivity and solvent type can change the suitable configuration.

Skipping Pilot Validation

Moving directly from a short lab test to production may leave important scale-up risks unresolved.

Undersizing Future Production

A system selected only for current demand may limit practical expansion and operating flexibility.

Ignoring Utilities and Plant Conditions

Energy, exhaust, water and installation space all influence final system design.

Ignoring Powder Collection

Particle size and powder behavior influence cyclone, filter and collection-system selection.

Avoid Costly Scale-Up Mistakes

Our engineers help you select the right scale and design a process that meets your powder quality goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a laboratory and pilot spray dryer?+

Laboratory systems support early feasibility and formulation work with limited material. Pilot systems support longer runs, process validation and higher-value scale-up data.

Can laboratory results be scaled directly to industrial production?+

Laboratory results provide an initial process direction, but pilot validation is often needed to confirm stability, recovery and longer-run behavior before industrial design.

How is the required evaporation capacity determined?+

It depends on feed rate, liquid content, solids concentration, target moisture, operating schedule and material behavior. Final capacity must be reviewed from actual process data.

How much material is needed for laboratory testing?+

The required quantity depends on the test objective, feed properties and number of operating conditions. Share the quantity available and an engineer can advise a practical test plan.

Can a pilot spray dryer be used for small-batch production?+

In some projects, a pilot system can support small-batch or sampling work. Suitability depends on operating duration, cleaning, controls and production requirements.

When is pilot validation necessary?+

Pilot validation is valuable when longer operating runs, powder recovery, process stability or industrial scale-up assumptions need to be confirmed.

Can an industrial system be expanded in the future?+

Future expansion should be discussed during engineering because chamber size, utilities, collection equipment, automation and plant layout can limit later changes.

What information is required before selecting a spray dryer?+

Useful information includes material name, feed form, solids, viscosity, solvent type, heat sensitivity, target powder, available quantity and expected production capacity.

When should a closed-loop spray dryer be considered?+

Closed-loop or inert-gas systems may be considered for applicable solvent-based or oxygen-sensitive processes after a formal material and safety review.

Do you provide installation and commissioning support?+

Installation guidance, commissioning scope, training and ongoing support can be discussed as part of the approved equipment project.
