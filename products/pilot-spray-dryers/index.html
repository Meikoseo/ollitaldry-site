FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the capacity range of the pilot spray dryer?+

The pilot series covers an indicative 5-30 L capacity range. Systems from 50-100+ L are treated as industrial-scale equipment. Final performance depends on feed solids, inlet conditions, target moisture and the selected configuration.

Which observation window should I choose?+

The small window supports compact routine inspection. The large window provides broader visibility for process observation and cleaning access. The right direction depends on model, material and operating objective.

Which atomization type is suitable for my product?+

Two-fluid and centrifugal atomization serve different feed rates, viscosity ranges and powder targets. Material information and laboratory results should be reviewed before selection.

Can I switch between different products?+

Yes, when process-contact materials, cleaning access and cross-contamination controls are configured for the intended campaigns.

What utilities are required?+

Electrical supply, process air, compressed air and the selected heating source must be confirmed for the final model and installation location.

Do you provide installation and training?+

Installation guidance, commissioning, operator training and remote engineering support can be included in the project scope.

Can pilot data be used for industrial scale-up?+

Pilot data provides a stronger basis for industrial selection by documenting feed behavior, drying stability, collection performance and operating limits under longer representative runs.

Can heat-sensitive materials be processed?+

Yes, subject to material evaluation. Inlet and outlet temperatures, residence time, atomization and collection direction are configured around the product sensitivity and powder target.

Are solvent-based feeds supported?+

Solvent feeds require an engineering and safety review. Closed-loop recovery, inerting, explosion protection and contact materials may be required depending on the solvent and process.

Can test spray services be arranged before purchase?+

A laboratory or pilot test can be discussed before equipment selection to evaluate feasibility, collect powder observations and define a more appropriate configuration direction.
