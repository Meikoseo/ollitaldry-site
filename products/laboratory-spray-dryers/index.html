2L Glass Chamber Model

Observe the Spray Drying Process Directly

The transparent borosilicate glass chamber gives researchers a clear view of atomization, drying and powder collection during small-scale feasibility work. It is a practical choice for universities, formulation laboratories and early process development.

  • Transparent borosilicate chamber for direct process observation
  • Up to 2 L/h water evaporation for laboratory development work
  • SUS316 two-fluid nozzle with SUS304 metal contact components
  • Compact 125 kg platform for laboratories and R&D facilities
  • Accessible glass components support inspection and routine cleaning
  • Can be used interchangeably with the stainless steel drying chamber
2L Stainless Steel Model

A More Durable 2L Laboratory Configuration

The stainless steel version keeps the same 2L laboratory-scale duty while replacing the glass drying chamber with a robust SUS304 process-contact configuration. Two observation windows provide visibility inside the chamber, while the metal construction supports corrosion-sensitive development work after material compatibility review.

  • SUS304 drying chamber for a durable process-contact direction
  • Suitable for corrosive feeds after material compatibility and safety review
  • Two chamber observation windows support in-process visual inspection
  • Up to 2 L/h water evaporation for laboratory development work
  • SUS316 two-fluid nozzle supplied as standard
  • Robust 145 kg platform for repeated laboratory operation
  • Full SUS316 process-contact construction available as a custom option
OLT-SD03L Laboratory Model

Evaluate Materials with Centrifugal Atomization

The OLT-SD03L extends laboratory testing to a 1.5-3.0 L/h processing range and supports centrifugal or two-fluid atomization. Its stainless steel drying system is intended for small-batch material evaluation where atomization behavior, rapid drying and powder recovery need to be reviewed before scale-up.

  • 1.5-3.0 L/h processing capacity for laboratory material evaluation
  • Centrifugal or two-fluid atomization direction based on the test objective
  • 30-300 °C inlet and 30-140 °C outlet temperature ranges
  • Downward co-current spray direction with 1.0-1.5 second drying time
  • SUS316 standard nozzle with SUS304 standard metal contact components
  • Full SUS316 process-contact construction available after engineering review
  • An extra-large chamber viewing window enables visual inspection during the process

Process Flow

How Laboratory Spray Drying Works

Each step is reviewed as part of one connected drying and powder-collection process.

01

Feed Preparation

Prepare and characterize the liquid feed before it enters the peristaltic pump.

02

Atomization

The SUS316 two-fluid nozzle converts the feed into fine, controlled droplets.

03

Rapid Drying

Heated process air removes moisture from the droplets inside the drying chamber.

04

Separation

The cyclone separates dried particles from the process air stream.

05

Exhaust & Filtration

Exhaust air follows the filtration or recovery direction selected for the material.

06

Powder Collection

Dry powder is collected for yield, moisture and particle evaluation.

Test Outputs & Engineering Review

What a Laboratory Test Should Tell You

The goal is not simply to make powder. A useful test connects measurable powder results with the equipment configuration and next development decision.

Collected Powder & Yield

Record recovered powder mass, collection behavior and practical dry-powder yield for the tested feed.

Moisture & Particle Analysis

Review residual moisture, particle-size distribution, morphology and powder consistency against the target.

Process Window & Repeatability

Relate feed rate, inlet and outlet temperatures, and atomization settings to stable repeatable operation.

Engineering Test Report

Document observations, recommended configuration and the next laboratory, pilot or scale-up decision.

FAQ

Laboratory Spray Dryer Questions

What materials can a laboratory spray dryer process?+

Water-based solutions, emulsions and suspensions are common starting points. Solvents, sticky feeds, corrosive materials and special powders require an engineering and safety review before equipment selection.

How much sample is required for an initial test?+

The required quantity depends on solids content, feed behavior, the selected configuration and the test objective. Share the available material quantity and our engineers will recommend a practical test plan.

Can laboratory results support pilot scale-up?+

Laboratory trials can establish feasibility and an initial operating window. Pilot validation is normally recommended when longer runs, representative collection performance or scale-up data are required.

Can heat-sensitive materials be evaluated?+

Yes, but the feed composition, thermal sensitivity, target moisture and residence-time requirements must be reviewed before defining temperatures and equipment options.

Are glass and stainless-steel chambers available?+

Both directions are available for selected laboratory configurations. The choice depends on process visibility, cleaning, containment, solvent and contact-material requirements.

Can the system be customized?+

Atomization, chamber material, collection, filtration, controls and auxiliary components can be evaluated around the material and development objective.

What particle size can a laboratory spray dryer achieve?+

Particle size depends on the feed properties, solids concentration, atomization method and operating conditions. Laboratory testing helps identify a practical particle-size range before pilot or production scale-up.

How long does a typical laboratory spray drying test take?+

Timing varies with sample preparation, test objectives and the number of operating conditions evaluated. After reviewing your material and targets, our team will provide a test scope and estimated schedule.

How is the laboratory spray dryer cleaned between batches?+

Product-contact parts are designed for practical access and cleaning. The recommended procedure depends on the material, chamber construction and contamination-control requirements.

Can the system handle small samples and low feed rates?+

Yes. Laboratory configurations are intended for development work with limited material, but the minimum practical sample quantity and feed rate depend on the selected atomizer, formulation and collection target.

What utilities are required to install a laboratory spray dryer?+

Typical requirements may include electrical power, compressed air and suitable exhaust or ventilation. Final utility specifications are confirmed for the selected model and process configuration.

Do you provide operator training and after-sales support?+

Yes. OLLITAL can support installation guidance, operator training, troubleshooting, spare parts and process consultation according to the equipment configuration and project location.
